# The 8-Expert Panel

Each expert contributes one lens to the pipeline in `SKILL.md`. These are
publicly documented principles drawn from each writer's published work and
widely taught frameworks — apply the principle, don't quote the person.

## 1. Eugene Schwartz — Awareness & Market Sophistication
*Breakthrough Advertising.* Copy must match the prospect's current state
of awareness (unaware → most aware) and the market's sophistication level
(how many "miracle claims" they've already heard and stopped believing).
A page that pitches a direct offer to an unaware reader, or makes a
first-generation miracle claim to a burned-out sophisticated market, fails
regardless of how well it's written. Used in: Step 2.

## 2. Claude Hopkins — Specificity & Testability
*Scientific Advertising.* Treat advertising as something to be measured,
not admired. Specific, verifiable detail ("9 out of 10," "in 11 minutes")
out-converts vague superlatives every time, because specifics read as
honest and superlatives read as salesmanship. Hopkins also pushed
"reason-why" copy — state the mechanism behind a claim, not just the
claim. Used in: Step 3 and as the bar for every claim in Step 7–8.

## 3. John Caples — Headline Mechanics
*Tested Advertising Methods.* Headlines are a science of mechanics, not
inspiration: self-interest, curiosity, news, how-to, question, and
proof/testimonial each trigger a different reflex. Caples' legacy is
treating every headline as a testable hypothesis with a specific
mechanical reason it should work. Used in: Step 4.

## 4. Dan Kennedy — Direct-Response Discipline
No-BS marketing principles: always ask for the sale, never bury the call
to action, use real deadlines and real scarcity (never fabricated), and
write with urgency because a reader who isn't moved to act today usually
never acts. Used in: Step 7 (offer/CTA framing).

## 5. Alex Hormozi — Value Equation & Belief Gaps
*$100M Offers* / *$100M Leads.* Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived
Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice). Stack
every component of an offer with a standalone value to make the price
look small by comparison. Separately, buyers don't act because of three
belief gaps: they doubt themselves (internal), doubt the type of solution
(vehicle), or doubt that outside forces will let it work (external). Used
in: Step 5 and Step 6.

## 6. Russell Brunson — Offer Stacking & Story Framing
*Expert Secrets* / *DotCom Secrets.* Wrap the offer in a narrative —
the "attractive character" arc (where the founder/expert was, what they
discovered, where they are now) — and reveal the offer as a stack
building to an obvious value gap versus price. Used in: Step 7
(narrative/stack framing).

## 7. Gary Halbert — Voice & Lead Mechanics
The most famous copywriter to ever write to "one reader, not a market."
Leads must hook within the first two sentences. Agitate the real problem
before introducing the mechanism or solution — readers buy relief from a
pain they've just been made to feel acutely. Conversational, blunt,
specific voice over polished corporate tone. Used in: Step 7 (lead and
voice).

## 8. Gary Bencivenga — Believability & Proof Density
Called by peers the "world's greatest living copywriter." His core
discipline: every claim needs a "because" — a reason, mechanism, or proof
point that answers the skeptical reader's instinctive "why should I
believe that?" before they ask it. Bencivenga maximized proof density —
stacking multiple independent proof types (data, testimonial, demonstration,
guarantee, authority) against the same core claim. Used in: Step 7 (claim
support) and Step 8 (proof audit).
